### [3] [Strain‐Engineered Fluoro‐Perovskites CsGeF 3 and CsSnF 3 for Efficient and Sustainable Solar Energy Conversion](https://doi.org/10.1002/ente.70574)
*N. El Hidaoui,F. Goumrhar,L. B. Drissi,R. Ahl Laamara*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: This first-principles study shows that hydrostatic compressive strain can substantially improve the electronic and optical properties of fluoroperovskites CsGeF3 and CsSnF3, making them more promising for solar absorption.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Fluorinated perovskites are much less explored than other halogenated perovskites for photovoltaic use, so the work aims to assess whether strain engineering can make CsGeF3 and CsSnF3 better solar absorber materials.

Method: The authors performed a comparative first-principles calculation on CsGeF3 and CsSnF3 under 2% and 4% hydrostatic compressive strain, then analyzed band structure, optical absorption, and photovoltaic performance using the spectroscopic limited maximum efficiency model.

Result: Under 4% compression, the band gap decreases to 1.9 eV for CsGeF3 and 1.32 eV for CsSnF3. The visible-light absorption coefficient increases strongly, and the estimated photovoltaic efficiency rises from 10.96% to 23.28% for CsGeF3 and from 25.19% to 30.64% for CsSnF3.

Conclusion: Compressive strain is an effective way to tune fluoroperovskites for solar applications, and both CsGeF3 and especially CsSnF3 show improved potential as efficient visible-light solar absorbers under strain.

Abstract: Although halogenated perovskites have been extensively studied for solar photovoltaic applications, fluorinated perovskites remain largely unexplored. In this context, we perform a first‐principles comparative study of the fluoro‐perovskite materials CsGeF 3 and CsSnF 3 . By applying hydrostatic compressive strains of 2% and 4%, we modulate their electronic structure. The results reveal a significant reduction in the band gap under 4% compressive stress, reaching 1.9 eV for CsGeF 3 and 1.32 eV for CsSnF 3 , which improves their suitability for visible‐light absorption. Additionally, the absorption coefficient in the visible range is strongly enhanced, indicating better light‐harvesting capabilities. Photovoltaic efficiencies, evaluated using the spectroscopic limited maximum efficiency model, increase from 10.96% to 23.28% for CsGeF 3 and from 25.19% to 30.64% for CsSnF 3 under 4% compression, confirming their promising potential as solar absorbers. These findings demonstrate that strain engineering is an effective strategy to tune the electronic and optical properties of fluoroperovskites, offering a practical pathway towards more efficient and sustainable solar energy conversion.
