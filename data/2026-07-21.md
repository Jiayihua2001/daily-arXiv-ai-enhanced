### [1] [Defect‐Driven Charge Separation in Ba‐Doped SrTiO 3 : Correlating Photoelectrochemical Performance With Dye‐Selective Photocatalysis](https://doi.org/10.1002/admi.70597)
*Sagar Nikam,Somnath Ladhane,Vikas P. Mhaske,G. H. Jain,S. Jadkar,S. Patole,G. E. Patil*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: Ba substitution in SrTiO3 nanoparticles improves lattice distortion, charge separation, and visible-light response, leading to much better photoelectrochemical and photocatalytic performance, with Ba0.5Sr0.5TiO3 showing the best activity and good cycling stability.

Motivation: The study aims to understand how Ba incorporation into SrTiO3 affects crystal structure, defect chemistry, and optoelectronic behavior, and whether these changes can enhance photoelectrochemical and photocatalytic performance under visible light.

Method: Ba x Sr 1-x TiO3 nanoparticles with several Ba contents were synthesized by a hydrothermal route. Structural properties were examined by X-ray diffraction, Williamson–Hall analysis, Raman spectroscopy, and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy. Optical and carrier dynamics were studied using absorption, photoluminescence, and time-resolved photoluminescence. Photoelectrochemical performance and photocatalytic dye degradation were then evaluated under visible-light irradiation, including radical-trapping and cycling tests.

Result: Ba incorporation caused single-phase perovskite formation, lattice expansion, increased distortion, slight band-gap narrowing, and defect-related electronic states. PL and TRPL indicated reduced recombination and longer carrier lifetime. The Ba0.5Sr0.5TiO3 sample delivered about 3.2 times higher photocurrent than pristine SrTiO3, degraded about 95% of methylene blue and nearly all Congo red within 180 min, followed pseudo-first-order kinetics, and retained about 93% activity after five cycles.

Conclusion: Moderate Ba substitution, especially at x = 0.5, optimizes the structural and electronic properties of SrTiO3, and the resulting lattice distortion and defect-assisted charge separation substantially enhance interfacial charge transfer and visible-light photocatalytic efficiency.

Abstract: Ba x Sr 1‐x TiO 3 (x = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, and 0.5) perovskite nanoparticles were synthesized via a hydrothermal route to investigate the effect of Ba substitution on the structural, photoelectrochemical (PEC), and photocatalytic properties of SrTiO 3 . X‐ray diffraction confirmed the formation of a single‐phase perovskite structure with systematic lattice expansion upon Ba incorporation, while Williamson–Hall analysis and Raman spectroscopy revealed enhanced lattice distortion and local structural modifications. Optical studies indicated slight band‐gap narrowing and improved visible‐light absorption, accompanied by defect‐related electronic perturbations. X‐ray photoelectron spectroscopy suggested the presence of defect‐related states, whereas photoluminescence and time‐resolved photoluminescence analyses indicated suppressed charge‐carrier recombination and prolonged carrier lifetime. PEC measurements demonstrated significantly improved charge‐transfer characteristics, with Ba 0.5 Sr 0.5 TiO 3 exhibiting a photocurrent density approximately 3.2 times higher than that of pristine SrTiO 3 . Photocatalytic studies under visible‐light irradiation showed that Ba 0.5 Sr 0.5 TiO 3 achieved ∼95% degradation of methylene blue and nearly complete degradation of Congo red within 180 min, following pseudo‐first‐order kinetics. Reactive‐species trapping experiments revealed dye‐dependent degradation pathways governed by •OH radicals and photogenerated holes. The optimized photocatalyst retained ∼93% of its activity after five cycles. The enhanced performance is attributed to the synergistic effects of lattice distortion, defect‐assisted charge separation, and improved interfacial charge transfer.
