### [2] [A Novel Mechanism of Optical Spectrum Formation in the Cs2NaInCl6 Doped with Sb and Er](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.5c03256)
*Inna A. Ivashchenko,Małgorzata Makowska-Janusik,Lubomir D. Gulay,Yu. Kazarinov,K. Lamonova,Y. Smortsova,A. Popov,K. Matras‐Postołek*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: The paper explains the optical spectrum and luminescence mechanism of Cs2NaInCl6 double halide perovskites, showing that a larger-than-expected bandgap and defect-driven In+ formation are central to their emission behavior, while Sb3+ and Er3+ doping modifies structure and absorption.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To clarify why Cs2NaInCl6 and its Sb3+/Er3+-doped variants exhibit luminescence, and to identify the roles of band structure, defects, and dopants in shaping their optical properties.

Method: Optical measurements were performed from 10 to 300 K at DESY, combined with density-functional theory (DFT) calculations to analyze bandgap, defect states, charge transfer, and the electronic origin of luminescence in undoped and doped Cs2NaInCl6 powders/microcrystals.

Result: The bandgap of Cs2NaInCl6 was found to be about 6.7 eV, larger than previously estimated. DFT indicates that luminescence is strongly linked to defect-induced formation of In+ optical centers via charge transfer from Cl− to In3+, creating defect-trapping levels and supporting a self-trapped exciton mechanism. Sb3+ and Er3+ doping alters the crystal structure and absorption characteristics.

Conclusion: The optical behavior of Cs2NaInCl6 is governed by a defect-assisted In+ center and self-trapped excitons, with dopants further tuning the material’s structure and absorption. This work provides a mechanistic basis for understanding and engineering the luminescence of double halide perovskites.

Abstract: We present a mechanism describing the formation of the optical spectrum in double halide perovskites (DHPs), specifically Sb- and Er-doped Cs2NaInCl6. Optical measurements conducted at the Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron (DESY) facility between 10 and 300 K have revealed that the bandgap of Cs2NaInCl6 is significantly larger than previously estimated, measuring 6.7 eV. Based on density-functional theory (DFT) calculations, we conclude that the formation of In+ (5s2 optical center) is crucial to the luminescence observed in Cs2NaInCl6 microcrystals produced by precipitation techniques. The formation of In+ is associated with the transfer of electron density from Cl– to In3+, which occurs due to defects in Cs2NaInCl6, resulting in increased In content. This process is linked to the self-trapped exciton mechanism and the formation of defect-trapping levels within the Cs2NaInCl6 bandgap. Additionally, embedding Sb3+ (5s2 doped ion) and Er3+ affects both the crystal structure and absorption properties of Cs2NaInCl6 samples. These findings shed light on the intricate interactions between the synthesis, composition, and the optical behavior of both undoped and Sb3+, Er3+-doped Cs2NaInCl6 powder samples.

### [4] [Modulating energy band structure and energy transfer pathways in CAU-10 series MOFs via ligand engineering to enhance photocatalytic oxidase-like activity for multimodal phytate detection.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bios.2026.118902)
*Wenhui Lu,Hanlin Sun,Ping Zhang,Xianghong Liu,Ran Liu,Cunyu Song,Long Jiang,Ping Ju,Fengli Qu,Ensheng Zhang*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: Ligand engineering was used to tune CAU-10 MOFs for photo-triggered oxidase-like catalysis, and CAU-10-NH2 showed the best activity, enabling multimodal phytic acid detection.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The study aimed to systematically understand how ligand substitution in CAU-10 MOFs affects band positions, charge/energy transfer, photoluminescence, and oxidase-mimicking photocatalysis, with the goal of building a sensitive analytical platform.

Method: A series of CAU-10 MOFs with different ligands (e.g., H, OH, Br, NO2, NH2) were prepared and compared under UV irradiation. Their band alignment, hydrophilicity, reactive oxygen species generation, and oxidase-like performance were evaluated. Mechanistic studies examined the roles of valence-band position, water interaction, and recombination behavior. The best-performing MOF was then integrated into a multimodal assay for phytic acid based on photocatalytic activity and ALP inhibition.

Result: CAU-10-NH2 exhibited the strongest photocatalytic oxidase-like activity. It generated mainly hydroxyl radicals at short irradiation times, with 1O2 and •O2- becoming more important with longer irradiation. The amino ligand improved both valence-band tuning and hydrophilicity, promoting hole-driven water oxidation. In contrast, CAU-10-H showed strong radiative recombination and negligible catalytic activity due to poor hydrophilicity. The CAU-10-NH2-based platform enabled multimodal detection of phytic acid and was applied to corn analysis.

Conclusion: Ligand engineering is an effective strategy to jointly control photoluminescence and photocatalytic oxidase-like behavior in CAU-10 MOFs. The amino-functionalized MOF is particularly effective because it balances favorable band alignment with enhanced water accessibility, making it useful for sensitive phytic acid sensing.

Abstract: The photocatalytic oxidase-like activity of the CAU-10 series metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) has not been systematically investigated to date. In this study, ligand engineering was employed to precisely tune the energy band positions of CAU-10 MOFs (CAU-10-H, -OH, -Br, -NO2, -NH2, etc.) and their energy transfer pathways in photo-triggered oxidase-mimicking reactions. Results showed that CAU-10-NH2 exhibits excellent photocatalytic oxidase-like activity, which was subsequently applied to the multimodal detection of phytic acid (PA). Under UV irradiation, CAU-10-NH2 generates ROS with •OH as the predominant species at short irradiation times (10-20 s), whereas the contributions of 1O2 and •O2- become significant alongside •OH upon extending irradiation to 3 min. Mechanistic investigations demonstrated that the amino group in the 5-aminoisophthalic acid ligand not only effectively regulates the valence band (VB) of the MOFs but also enhances their hydrophilicity through hydrogen bonding with water, thereby facilitating hole oxidation to generate abundant hydroxyl radicals (•OH). In contrast, although the isophthalic acid-based MOF (CAU-10-H) exhibits a suitable VB alignment, its inherently weak hydrophilicity impedes hole-water interactions, leading to the dominant radiative recombination (strong phosphorescence) and consequently negligible photocatalytic oxidase-like activity. Collectively, these results highlight how ligand engineering dictates both the photoluminescent and photocatalytic oxidase-like activity of CAU-10 MOFs. Moreover, leveraging the excellent photocatalytic oxidase-like activity of CAU-10-NH2 and the significant inhibitory effect of phytic acid (PA) on alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity, a multimodal PA detection method has been developed and applied for the analysis of PA content in corn.
