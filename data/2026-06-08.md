### [1] [Reactive reconstruction and embedded passivation of heterointerfaces for intrinsically stable perovskite photovoltaics](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aef6596)
*Zhixin Ren,Jike Ding,Yuan Qiu,Huanqi Cao,Cong Chen,Shifu Zhang,U. Paetzold,Jinzhao Li,Qian Zhao,Liying Yang,Shougen Yin,Jijian Lian*

Main category: Property prediction & informatics

TL;DR: The paper addresses heterointerface degradation in perovskite solar cells by comparing bulky anionic and cationic passivators, then introduces cesium pyridine-3-carboxylate as a thermally robust passivation strategy that improves efficiency and operational stability.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Perovskite solar cells suffer from interfacial aging under thermal and operational stress, which limits long-term device durability. Conventional passivators can be thermally unstable, motivating the search for interface treatments that remain effective during prolonged operation.

Method: The authors studied aging behavior at heterointerfaces for bulky anionic versus cationic passivators, identified lattice expansion induced by anionic species under thermal stress, and synthesized cesium pyridine-3-carboxylate as a new passivator. This compound was used to promote reactive surface reconstruction, suppress ion migration, and enhance interfacial charge transfer in devices.

Result: Devices with this passivation strategy reached a certified power conversion efficiency of 27.28%. Unencapsulated devices retained 87% of initial efficiency after 4092 hours of maximum power point tracking under ISOS-L-1, and encapsulated devices retained 80% after 3566 hours at 85°C under ISOS-L-2.

Conclusion: The work shows that passivator charge type strongly affects interfacial thermal stability and that cesium pyridine-3-carboxylate provides a practical route to more durable perovskite solar cells. It also offers design principles for stabilizing heterointerfaces and improving operational lifetime.

Abstract: Heterointerface degradation under operational stress represents a critical limitation to perovskite solar cell longevity. Here, we demonstrate contrasting aging behaviors between bulky anionic and cationic passivators at heterointerfaces, wherein anionic species induce lattice expansion under thermal stress. To address the thermal instability of conventional passivators, we synthesized cesium pyridine-3-carboxylate, which triggers reactive surface reconstruction, suppresses ion migration, and enhances interfacial charge transfer. Devices using this passivation strategy achieved certified power conversion efficiencies of 27.28% with exceptional operational stability. Unencapsulated devices retained 87% of their initial efficiency after 4092 hours of maximum power point tracking under the ISOS-L-1 protocol, while encapsulated devices maintained 80% of their initial efficiency after 3566 hours at 85°C under the ISOS-L-2 protocol. These findings establish rational design principles for stabilizing perovskite heterointerfaces and advancing device durability.

### [2] [More π, less Gap: Band Gap Tuning in Hybrid Antimony Halide Charge Transfer Semiconductors](https://doi.org/10.1002/zaac.70153)
*Jakob Blahusch,M. Zipkat,W. Schnick,B. Lotsch*

Main category: Property prediction & informatics

TL;DR: The paper shows that changing the organic cation in a hybrid organic–inorganic antimony chloride can significantly tune the band gap through charge-transfer interactions, with a more π-conjugated cation producing a narrower gap.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Hybrid organic–inorganic charge-transfer semiconductors are promising for band-gap engineering, but the role of the organic cation's π-system has been insufficiently explored. The study aims to test whether extending π-conjugation in the cation can systematically modify optical properties.

Method: The authors synthesized and characterized (phenH2)SbCl5 and compared it with the previously reported (2,2-bpyH2)SbCl5. They used diffuse-reflectance spectroscopy to measure optical absorption and density-functional theory (DFT) calculations to analyze the electronic structure and orbital contributions.

Result: Both compounds contain tetrameric [Sb4Cl20]8− units and show charge-transfer band gaps. The phenanthrolinium compound exhibits a red-shifted absorption onset and a reduced band gap of 2.1 eV versus 2.6 eV for the bipyridinium analogue, a decrease of more than 0.4 eV. DFT attributes this shift to greater phenanthroline π* contribution to the conduction band.

Conclusion: Increasing the π-conjugation of the organic cation is an effective strategy for lowering the band gap in hybrid organic–inorganic antimony halide charge-transfer semiconductors, enabling rational optical tuning through cation design.

Abstract: Organic cations can directly contribute to the electronic structure in hybrid organic–inorganic (HOI) materials, resulting in the formation of charge transfer semiconductors. Systematic tuning of the band gap by varying the organic cation in HOI charge transfer semiconductors remains underexplored. Here, we report the synthesis and characterization of (phenH 2 )SbCl 5 (phenH 2 = 1,10‐phenanthrolinium) and investigate how the extended π‐system influences optical properties compared to the previously reported (2,2‐bpyH 2 )SbCl 5 (2,2‐bpyH 2 = 2,2′‐bipyridinium). Both compounds feature tetrameric [Sb 4 Cl 20 ] 8‐ units and exhibit charge–transfer band gaps. Diffuse‐reflectance spectroscopy shows a red‐shifted absorption onset for (phenH 2 )SbCl 5 relative to (2,2‐bpyH 2 )SbCl 5 . The band gap decreases from 2.6 eV in (2,2‐bpyH 2 )SbCl 5 to 2.1 eV in (phenH 2 )SbCl 5 , a reduction of >0.4 eV. Density‐functional theory (DFT) calculations indicate that this red‐shift arises from increased contribution of the phenanthroline π*‐orbitals to the conduction band. This demonstrates that systematic variation of the organic cation's π‐conjugation can provide a strategy for band gap tuning in charge–transfer HOI antimony halides.

Method: The study introduces a synergistic binary additive approach: semicarbazide hydrochloride acts as a crystallization regulator, while N-fluorobenzenesulfonimide coordinates with undercoordinated Sn2+/Pb2+ via sulfonyl coordination and pi-metal interactions to delay crystallization, improve crystal orientation, and passivate defects.

As a result, HTL‐free mixed Sn–Pb PSCs achieve a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.73% with a high open‐circuit voltage ( V OC ) of 0.877 V, together with excellent storage stability retaining over 91% of the initial efficiency after 1500 h. This work demonstrates an effective molecular‐level strategy for overcoming the intrinsic limitations of HTL‐free Sn–Pb perovskites, offering a viable pathway toward simplified and stable photovoltaic architectures.

### [5] [Investigation of the electronic, magnetic, and thermoelectric characteristics of the transition metal-based double perovskites Ba2XNbO6 (X = V, Cr, Mn, and Fe) for spintronic applications](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6ra02067d)
*S. Rafique,Q. Mahmood,Ghulam M. Mustafa,H. Alkhaldi,A. El-Moula,Awatif Alshamari,I. Boukhris,Mohd Taukeer Khan*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: The paper uses first-principles calculations to show that Ba2XNbO6 (X = V, Cr, Mn, Fe) are structurally stable and promising for spintronics and thermoelectrics, with room-temperature ferromagnetism and strong performance variations driven by B-site substitution.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To identify double perovskite oxides with high Curie temperatures, spin-polarized electronic structures, and useful thermoelectric response for potential spintronic applications.

Method: First-principles calculations were performed with WIEN2k to evaluate structural stability, formation enthalpy, electronic band structure, magnetic properties, exchange parameters, Curie temperature, and thermoelectric quantities.

Result: All compounds are thermodynamically and structurally stable. Estimated Curie temperatures are 289-325 K, supporting room-temperature ferromagnetism. Ba2VNbO6 and Ba2MnNbO6 are half-metallic ferromagnets, while Ba2CrNbO6 and Ba2FeNbO6 are ferromagnetic semiconductors. Magnetic moments vary from 2.0 to 5.0 micro-B per formula unit, and Ba2VNbO6 shows the best thermoelectric performance with a power factor of 8.60 W mK−2.

Conclusion: Substituting the B-site ion in Ba2XNbO6 effectively tunes electronic structure, magnetism, and thermoelectric behavior, making these materials promising candidates for room-temperature spintronic and thermoelectric devices.

Abstract: Spin-polarized materials with high Curie temperatures are emerging aspirants for spintronics. In the present work, we conduct first-principles calculations using the WIEN2k code to examine the structural, electronic, magnetic, and thermoelectric properties of Ba2XNbO6 (X = V, Cr, Mn, and Fe). The thermodynamic stability is confirmed by the negative enthalpies of formation and the structural stability by the tolerance factor. The range of Curie temperatures (Tc) from 289 K to 325 K for the studied materials allows for room-temperature ferromagnetism. Inferring from their spin-polarized band structures, Ba2VNbO6 and Ba2MnNbO6 have half-metallic ferromagnetism, and Ba2CrNbO6 and Ba2FeNbO6 have ferromagnetic semiconductor behavior. The calculated exchange and crystal field energies and exchange constants reveal dominant spin–orbit coupling rather than magnetic ion clustering. The total magnetic moment analysis demonstrates that the magnetic moment ranges from 2.0µB to 5.00µB for the Fe-based compounds, reflecting the gradual addition of electrons to the 3d orbitals, and its distribution to non-magnetic sites confirms the exchange of electrons. Additionally, these compounds show great potential in thermoelectrics. Ba2VNbO6 reached a power factor of 8.60 W mK−2 because of its good electrical conductivity and moderate Seebeck coefficient. These results underscore that substituting the B-site elements in Ba2XNbO6 (X = V, Cr, Mn, and Fe) can improve the spintronic and thermoelectric properties by modifying the electronic structure, magnetic behavior, and measurable thermoelectric power.

Main category: Other

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The work is motivated by the growing interest in halide perovskites for energy storage and optoelectronics, and by the limited number of studies on CsSrI3 despite its potential for high-performance dielectric and capacitor applications.

Method: This is a review paper that surveys recent progress on Si-doped CsSrI3 perovskites, focusing on fabrication, dielectric behavior, charge storage mechanisms, and their suitability as dielectric chips in MIM capacitors.

Result: The review summarizes existing findings suggesting that Si doping can tune charge storage in CsSrI3-based materials and improve their relevance for capacitor applications, while also identifying that the research field remains underexplored.

Conclusion: Si-doped CsSrI3 perovskites are promising candidates for sustainable MIM capacitor dielectrics, but further experimental and device-level studies are needed to address open challenges and establish practical applications.

Abstract: Halide perovskites have emerged as the current trending materials for applications in energy storage and other high-performance optoelectronic devices due to their charge-carrier dynamics, low defect density, enhanced breakdown voltage and high dielectric profiles. Si-doped materials, such as oxides and perovskites, play a significant role in high-efficiency capacitors because of the tunable charge storage mechanisms enabled by Si. Regulating the fabrication of Si-doped CsSrI3 dielectric materials is a crucial step to realize a practical metal-insulator-metal (MIM) capacitor with highly sustainable characteristics. Despite their potential, a recent literature survey shows that very few works have been performed on CsSrI3 perovskites, prompting further research on their various properties, especially in the context of charge storage. This review highlights the recent progress in Si-doped CsSrI3 perovskites as dielectric chips for MIM capacitor applications. The remaining challenges in the research on Si-based CsSrI3 perovskite optoelectronic devices are also discussed.
