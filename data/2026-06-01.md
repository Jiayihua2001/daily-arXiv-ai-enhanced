### [1] [Diastereomeric Configuration Modulates Liquid–Liquid Phase Separation and Catalysis in Minimalist Dipeptide Coacervates](https://doi.org/10.1002/ange.9390195)
*Shuai Peng,Xiaokun Zhang,Xin Shi,Meng Yu,Shoupeng Cao,Ning Gao*

Main category: CSP & polymorphs

TL;DR: The paper shows that the stereochemistry of minimalist dipeptide isomers can determine whether they form liquid coacervates or crystals, thereby controlling protocell-like compartmentalization and reaction acceleration.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how molecular chirality influences liquid–liquid phase separation in small-molecule systems relevant to origin-of-life protocell models, a topic that has been largely unexplored.

Method: The authors studied four dipeptide stereoisomers built from L/D-proline and L/D-naphthylalanine under identical aqueous conditions, then used phase-behavior assays, guest-molecule uptake tests, stereoselective reaction measurements, single-crystal X-ray diffraction, and all-atom molecular dynamics simulations.

Result: Two stereoisomers, LPDNal and DPLNal, underwent LLPS and formed coacervates that concentrated guest molecules and accelerated stereoselective reactions. The other two, LPLNal and DPDNal, favored crystallization instead. Structural analysis linked this split behavior to stereochemistry-dependent differences in hydrogen bonding and aromatic stacking.

Conclusion: Diastereomeric configuration can directly regulate whether a minimalist molecular system undergoes LLPS or crystallization, demonstrating that stereochemistry is a key design principle for protocell-like compartment formation and function.

Abstract: Although coacervates formed via liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS) of small molecules are widely recognized as plausible protocell models relevant to the origin of life, how the stereochemistry of small molecules influences LLPS remains largely unexplored. Here we report a set of minimalist dipeptide stereoisomers composed of l ‐proline ( L P ) or d ‐proline ( D P ), and l ‐naphthylalanine ( L Nal ), or d ‐naphthylalanine ( D Nal ), in which chirality modulates the propensity for LLPS under identical aqueous conditions. Specifically, L P D Nal and D P L Nal independently undergo LLPS to form coacervates that selectively accumulate diverse guest molecules and act as efficient crucibles, markedly accelerating stereoselective reactions. By contrast, the remaining stereoisomers, L P L Nal and D P D Nal , preferentially access a competing crystallization pathway. Single‐crystal x‐ray diffraction and all‐atom molecular dynamics simulations reveal that this divergence originates from stereochemistry‐dependent variations in intermolecular hydrogen‐bonding patterns and aromatic stacking. Together, these findings establish diastereomeric configuration‐modulated LLPS in a minimalist molecular system and demonstrate how molecular stereochemistry can directly regulate protocell‐like compartmentalization.

Abstract: The study presents an intelligent Bayesian optimization framework with semantic-aware decision-making for designing coupled material–process energy conversion systems. ### [3] [On mechanism of phase stabilization in ferroelectric and high- k HfO2](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0310587)
*S. Barabash,Huazhi Fang*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: The study uses first-principles calculations to show that dopant-induced ferroelectric stabilization in HfO2 cannot be explained by bulk thermodynamics alone; deposition history, dopant arrangement, and thin-film effects are likely essential.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The authors aim to understand why certain dopants appear to stabilize ferroelectric HfO2 phases experimentally, despite bulk energetics often favoring non-ferroelectric polymorphs. A key challenge is the strong dependence of defect energetics on dopant-vacancy configuration.

Method: They develop a multi-stage screening workflow to find low-energy dopant-vacancy configurations and compare two models: an equilibrated-solution model representing bulk-like doping and a planar model representing atomic-layer-deposition-like dopant layering. They evaluate 0 K energetics, configurational entropy, and vibrational contributions, with phonon calculations used to benchmark vibrational effects.

Result: In the bulk equilibrated-solution model, ionically compensated doping is generally insufficient to make the orthorhombic ferroelectric phase more stable than the monoclinic phase, and some dopants worsen its relative stability. Entropy effects are small, and vibrational effects mainly favor the tetragonal phase without changing the overall phase ordering at moderate temperatures. Many rhombohedral-ferroelectric supercells relax into orthorhombic-like structures. The planar deposition model increases sensitivity to dopant identity and can alter phase competition.

Conclusion: Ferroelectric stabilization in doped HfO2 thin films likely depends on interfacial and finite-size effects together with non-equilibrium dopant distributions created during deposition, rather than on bulk dopant thermodynamics alone.

Abstract: We present a systematic first-principles study of dopant-induced phase stabilization in HfO2 across ferroelectric (FE) and non-ferroelectric polymorphs for a large set of dopants. To overcome the strong configuration dependence of defect energetics, we develop a multi-stage screening workflow that identifies low-energy dopant–vacancy configurations. In an equilibrated-solution [“PVD” (physical vapor deposition)] model, bulk T = 0 K energetics indicate that ionically compensated doping alone is insufficient to stabilize the orthorhombic-ferroelectric phase over the monoclinic phase; certain dopants even increase its relative energy. Configurational-entropy corrections are small at device-relevant temperatures, and vibrational contributions—benchmarked against explicit phonon calculations—primarily stabilize the tetragonal phase, remaining inadequate to reverse bulk phase ordering at moderate anneal temperatures. We further observe that many rhombohedral-FE supercells lose phase identity upon relaxation, typically collapsing toward orthorhombic-FE motifs, underscoring sensitivity to local defect arrangements. To assess deposition effects, we introduce a planar (“atomic layer deposition”) model that mimics dopant layering; it amplifies dopant-identity sensitivity and can reshape phase competition compared to the equilibrated-solution limit. Overall, our results suggest that experimentally observed ferroelectric stabilization in thin films arises from a combination of interfacial/finite-size terms and deposition-induced dopant distributions, rather than bulk thermodynamics alone.

Motivation: To advance quantum and polaritonic technologies by achieving and understanding spinor condensates at room temperature, rather than only at cryogenic temperatures.

Method: The authors use a lead-halide perovskite microcavity under nonresonant optical excitation, observe polarization-resolved condensation behavior, and interpret the dynamics with a spin-dependent Gross-Pitaevskii model.

Abstract: A deeper understanding of room temperature polariton condensed phases is essential for advancing quantum applications. Spin degrees of freedom inherent in polariton particles manifest themselves in the form of spinor condensation, which has been demonstrated only at cryogenic temperatures in the past. Herein, we demonstrate room-temperature spinor polariton condensation in a lead-halide perovskite microcavity under nonresonant optical excitation. The crystalline anisotropy of the perovskite induces a linear polarization splitting of the lower polariton modes, and, above the condensation threshold, nonlinear polariton-polariton interactions drive the formation of elliptically polarized condensates. The condensation dynamics are analyzed using a spin-dependent Gross-Pitaevskii model, which provides a qualitative framework for understanding the experimentally observed polarization evolution and two-stage threshold behavior. Our findings pave the way for all-optical control of polariton spin states at room temperature, opening a path toward scalable polaritonic quantum devices.
