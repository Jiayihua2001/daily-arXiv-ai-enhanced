Method: A combined computational approach was used: static Density Functional Theory to evaluate adsorption sites and binding energies, and Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics at 298 K to test thermal stability and ion mobility.

Abstract: The interaction of lithium (Li+) ions with the HKUST-1 Metal-Organic Framework was investigated using a comprehensive computational approach combining static Density Functional Theory (DFT) and Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics (AIMD). Electronic structure calculations identify the copper paddlewheel node as the thermodynamically preferred adsorption site, where the Li+ ion binds to the carboxylate oxygen atoms with a binding energy of -5.50 eV, significantly surpassing interactions at the organic linkers. AIMD simulations at 298 K confirm the thermal stability of this configuration, revealing that the Li+ ion is spatially trapped within the potential well of the paddlewheel. The cation exhibits localized rattling motion restricted by a rigid bidentate coordination geometry (Li–O of 1.90 Å), with no long-range diffusion observed on the picosecond timescale. Furthermore, the intercalation of lithium induces bandgap narrowing. These findings establish the atomic-level mechanisms governing the structural confinement and electronic modulation of HKUST-1 upon lithiation, providing critical insights for its potential application in electrochemical systems.
