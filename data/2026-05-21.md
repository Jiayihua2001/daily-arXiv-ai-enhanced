### [1] [Two-Dimensional Valley-Polarized Antiferromagnetic Metal-Organic Frameworks via Cluster Assembly and Ligand Modulation](https://doi.org/10.1088/1674-1056/ae6d98)
*Changjie Lian,Ping Guo,Jiming Zheng,Lei Shen*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: The paper proposes a design strategy for intrinsic antiferromagnetic valleytronic materials using 2D metal-organic frameworks built from magnetic TMCu4 clusters on a honeycomb-Kagome lattice, showing strong valley polarization and tunable magnetic anisotropy via ligand choice.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Antiferromagnetic valleytronics is attractive for high-density, low-crosstalk information processing, but intrinsic AFM valley systems are difficult to realize. The work aims to provide a practical materials-design route to overcome this limitation.

Method: The authors construct a series of 2D MOFs by assembling triangular bipyramidal transition-metal-copper clusters (TMCu4, TM = W, Os) and connecting them with different organic ligands (C4H4P2, N2C2H2, N2C8H4) on a honeycomb-Kagome lattice. They then use first-principles calculations to evaluate magnetic anisotropy and valley splitting.

Result: The C4H4P2-bridged framework shows strong out-of-plane magnetic anisotropy (11 meV/unit) and large spontaneous valley polarization (143.3 meV). Replacing the ligand with N2C2H2 or N2C8H4 changes the easy axis from out-of-plane to in-plane, but valley splitting can still be induced by an external perpendicular magnetic field.

Conclusion: The study demonstrates that magnetic cluster assembly plus ligand regulation is an effective strategy for engineering AFM valley polarization in 2D MOFs, offering a theoretical basis and candidate platform for AFM valleytronic devices.

Abstract: Antiferromagnetic (AFM) valleytronics presents a highly promising paradigm for high-density, crosstalk-free information processing. However, realizing intrinsic AFM valley systems remains a significant challenge. In this study, based on the two-dimensional crystal structure of the honeycomb-Kagome lattice (HKL), we propose a design strategy for achieving AFM valley polarization through magnetic cluster assembly and ligand regulation. By using triangular bipyramidal transition metal-copper clusters TMCu 4 (TM = W, Os) as magnetic units and bridging them with organic ligands (C 4 H 4 P 2 , N 2 C 2 H 2 , N 2 C 8 H 4 ), we constructed a series of two-dimensional metal-organic frameworks (2D MOFs). First-principles calculations reveal that the framework bridged by C 4 H 4 P 2 exhibits significant out-of-plane magnetic anisotropy (11 meV/unit) and large spontaneous valley polarization (143.3 meV). When the ligand is replaced with N 2 C 2 H 2 or N 2 C 8 H 4 , the easy magnetization axis shifts from out-of-plane to in-plane. Nevertheless, valley splitting can still occur under a perpendicular magnetic field. This study offers a theoretical framework and a material platform for the design of AFM valleytronic devices.

### [3] [REASSESSING THE ROLE OF ITO THICKNESS TO OPTIMISE THE DEVICE PERFORMANCE IN PEROVSKITE SOLAR CELLS](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6528/ae6dcc)
*Nilanjeeb Das,Satyabrata Guruprasad,Ashish Malik,Pilik Basumatary,Dhriti Sundar Ghosh*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: The paper studies how indium tin oxide (ITO) thickness affects planar n-i-p perovskite solar cell performance, finding that intermediate thicknesses improve optical interference, light absorption, and electrical output, with 150 nm giving the highest current density and 100 nm giving the best overall efficiency.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: ITO thickness is usually treated as fixed because of commercial standardization, but it can strongly influence optical losses, carrier generation, and resistive behavior in perovskite solar cells. The study aims to identify an optimal thickness that balances performance and cost.

Method: A combined opto-electrical simulation was performed for planar n-i-p perovskite solar cells with ITO thicknesses of 70, 100, 150, and 190 nm. Optical behavior was analyzed using the transfer-matrix method to evaluate interference, transmission, and absorption, and electrical simulations were used to assess carrier transport and device metrics.

Result: Intermediate ITO thicknesses of 100-150 nm produced constructive interference, improved visible-light transmission, and increased absorption in the perovskite layer. The 150 nm ITO device achieved the highest short-circuit current density of 21.42 mA cm-2 and a PCE of 22.67%. The 100 nm device showed 21.25 mA cm-2 and the highest PCE of 23.23%, while the 70 nm device reached 22.90% PCE.

Conclusion: ITO thickness is an important and underexplored design parameter in perovskite solar cells. Optimizing it can improve optical and electrical performance while offering a cost-efficient route for device design, with 100-150 nm emerging as the most favorable range.

Abstract: Perovskite solar cells (PvSCs) are promising next-generation photovoltaic devices due to their high efficiency and low fabrication cost. Indium tin oxide (ITO), used as a transparent electrode in solar cells, significantly impacts device performance, however, its thickness is unaccounted for due to standardised commercial availability. This study systematically investigates the role of ITO thickness in planar n-i-p perovskite solar cells using a combined opto-electrical simulation. Different ITO thicknesses (70, 100, 150, and 190 nm) were analysed to understand and explore thickness dependent optical interference and dissipative absorptions, and their resultant effect on carrier generation, charge transport, and overall device performance. Optical simulations using the transfer-matrix method show that constructive interference builds up at intermediate ITO thicknesses (100-150 nm), which in turn enhances the visible-light transmission and increases light absorption within the perovskite layer. Electrical simulations confirm the results of optical simulations, which yield higher short-circuit current density, improved fill factor, and reduced resistive losses. Devices with 150 nm ITO exhibit the highest short circuit current density of 21.42 mAcm-2 and PCE of 22.67%, while those with 70 and 100 nm show short-circuit current density of 21.21 mAcm-2 and 21.25 mAcm-2 with PCE of 22.90% and 23.23% respectively, presenting a promising cost-efficient guidance to researchers without much compromise in the performance. This study exemplifies that ITO thickness is a critical, still underexplored design parameter, and its optimisation provides a guiding route for achieving high efficiency of photovoltaic devices, while reducing material costs for next-generation photovoltaics.

### [4] [Machine Learning‐Assisted Inverse Design of Soft and Multifunctional Hybrid Liquid Metal Composites](https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.75719)
*Lijun Zhou,Yunsik Ohm,Ren-Mian Chin,Olivia Kerr,Krithika Manohar,M. Malakooti*

Main category: Property prediction & informatics

TL;DR: The paper proposes an ML-assisted inverse design framework for hybrid liquid metal composites, using physics-based data generation plus machine learning and Bayesian optimization to efficiently discover multifunctional soft materials with targeted thermal, mechanical, and density properties.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional trial-and-error optimization of hybrid filler composites is slow, expensive, and wasteful, especially when balancing competing requirements such as thermal conductivity, elasticity, and low density for wearable and stretchable electronics.

Method: The authors combine a physics-based homogenization model to generate a large synthetic dataset with machine learning models, including Random Forest regression and a generative model, then apply Bayesian optimization to search roughly 690,000 candidate formulations across different polymers and solid fillers for inverse design.

Result: The framework identified an optimized composite with thermal conductivity of about 2.4 W/(m·K), roughly 1.6 times higher than the liquid-metal composite and 12 times higher than the polymer, while preserving low-modulus, high-strain behavior (0.93 MPa, 155% strain) and lowering cost. Device demonstrations showed improved thermal management and energy harvesting in flexible electronics and thermoelectrics.

Conclusion: This study shows that combining physics-based modeling with ML and Bayesian optimization can enable practical, low-waste inverse design of multifunctional composites, and the approach is generalizable to other material systems and property targets.

Abstract: Soft functional materials are essential for wearables and stretchable electronics to meet multiple performance demands. Hybrid filler composites (HFCs) with liquid and solid inclusions offer tailored properties. However, identifying optimal compositions through conventional trial‐and‐error is costly, inefficient, and generates substantial material waste. We present an inverse design framework for hybrid liquid metal composites that combines data generation from a physics‐based homogenization model with machine learning (ML) algorithms and Bayesian optimization to enable intelligent design of experiments. This framework explores ∼690 000 composite formulations across diverse polymers and solid fillers, revealing key composition–property relationships while achieving targeted optimization of thermal conductivity, elasticity, and density with minimal material use. Comparative studies between Random Forest regression and a generative model provide practical strategies for identifying synthesizable composites. An inversely designed HFC achieves a thermal conductivity of ∼2.4 W/(m·K), 1.6 × that of the liquid‐metal composite and 12 × that of the polymer, while maintaining low‐modulus, high‐strain mechanics (0.93 MPa, 155% strain) at reduced cost. Integration into flexible electronics and thermoelectrics demonstrates enhanced thermal management and energy harvesting. This work establishes a practical, ML‐assisted inverse design paradigm for guiding the discovery of multifunctional composites, readily extendable to other material systems and properties.
