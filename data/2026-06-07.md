### [3] [Theoretical investigation of Rb-based double halide perovskites for photovoltaic, photocatalytic water splitting, and CO2 reduction applications](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6ra02841a)

Main category: Other

TL;DR: This study uses DFT and SCAPS-1D to evaluate lead-free Rb2NaRhX6 (X = F, Cl, Br, I) double halide perovskites, finding them structurally stable, direct-band-gap semiconductors with strong optical absorption and promising photocatalytic and photovoltaic properties. Rb2NaRhCl6 gives the best simulated solar-cell performance, with a PCE of 27.03%.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The work aims to identify stable, lead-free perovskite materials with favorable electronic, optical, photocatalytic, and photovoltaic characteristics for next-generation optoelectronic and energy-conversion applications.

Method: The authors combined density functional theory calculations with SCAPS-1D device simulations. They assessed crystal stability, formation energies, mechanical and dynamical stability, electronic band structures using PBE, SOC, and HSE06, optical absorption, band-edge alignment for water splitting and CO2 reduction, pH-dependent redox behavior, and photovoltaic device performance in an FTO/SnS2/Rb2NaRhX6/Au architecture.

Result: All four compounds are predicted to be stable cubic double perovskites with direct band gaps that decrease from F to I. Br- and I-based materials show stronger visible-light absorption. Rb2NaRhF6, Rb2NaRhCl6, and Rb2NaRhBr6 are suitable for photocatalytic water splitting, and all four are favorable for CO2 reduction. In SCAPS-1D, Rb2NaRhCl6 performs best, reaching 27.03% PCE, 0.893 V VOC, 35.41 mA cm−2 JSC, and 83.56% FF.

Abstract: Understanding the intrinsic properties of emerging materials is essential for advancing next-generation optoelectronic and energy-related technologies, and DFT offers an effective route for this purpose. In this work, the structural, electronic, optical, photocatalytic, and photovoltaic properties of lead-free Rb2NaRhX6 (X = F, Cl, Br, I) double halide perovskites are systematically investigated using a combined DFT and SCAPS-1D simulation approach. Structural analysis confirms that all compounds crystallize in stable cubic phases and satisfy thermodynamic, mechanical, and dynamical stability criteria through negative formation energies, Born stability conditions, and the absence of imaginary phonon frequencies. Electronic band-structure calculations including PBE, spin–orbit coupling (SOC) and HSE06 hybrid functional corrections reveal direct band-gap semiconducting behavior with a systematic reduction in band gap from fluoride to iodide compositions.
