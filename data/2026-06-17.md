Main category: Other

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>

XPS and Fourier-transform infrared after cycling confirm complete Li2O2 reversibility and suppressed carbonate byproducts. The band alignment straddles the O2/O2- and Li2O2/O2 redox potentials, enabling photogenerated holes to directly oxidize Li2O2 and electrons to drive oxygen reduction reaction (ORR).

<details>

Motivation: The work aims to discover efficient, low-cost electrocatalysts for key energy-conversion reactions in fuel cells, water splitting, and metal-air batteries, where high activity and selectivity for ORR, OER, and HER are essential.

Method: The authors used density functional theory to evaluate 30 transition metals anchored to TAP monolayers as single-atom catalysts. Reaction energetics were analyzed with the computational hydrogen electrode model under idealized acidic conditions, and mechanistic interpretation included spin density, charge density difference, and electronic structure analyses.

Result: For the 4e− pathway, Zn-TAP shows the best ORR performance with an overpotential of 0.43 V, while Rh-TAP is best for OER with 0.58 V; Fe-TAP also performs well for ORR at 0.58 V. We note that this study is based on the computational hydrogen electrode (CHE) model under idealized acidic conditions (pH = 0) without explicit or implicit solvent effects. Therefore, the calculated overpotentials and free-energy profiles represent a thermodynamic screening framework, and extension to neutral/alkaline or experimental environments requires additional consideration of solvation and pH effects. Overall, this work highlights the potential of TMTAP SACs and offers guidance for the rational design of such catalysts.

### [8] [Chiral molecule-induced interface passivation for high-efficiency and stable perovskite solar cells.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcis.2026.140925)
*Yixuan Gao,Hua Li,Yulu Wang,Xiaoqi Fan,Enyu Liu,Yingjia Zhuansun,Jiating Wang,Decai Zhu,Yuchen Liu,Qingbo Wei*

Main category: Property prediction & informatics

TL;DR: Chiral BINAPO molecules were used to post-treat SnO2 electron transport layers, where their PO groups passivated interfacial defects and improved charge extraction in perovskite solar cells. The S-enantiomer performed better, enabling a 16.27% efficiency HTL-free carbon-based device and strong humidity stability.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Buried interface defects in perovskite solar cells limit electron transport, increase non-radiative recombination, and reduce both efficiency and stability. The work aims to improve SnO2/perovskite interfacial quality through molecular passivation.

Method: Two chiral molecules, R-BINAPO and S-BINAPO, were designed and applied as post-treatments on SnO2 ETLs. Their phosphine oxide groups coordinated with undercoordinated Sn4+ on SnO2 and Pb2+ at the perovskite interface, while the two enantiomers were compared to assess stereochemistry-dependent passivation effects.

Result: S-BINAPO showed stronger defect passivation than R-BINAPO, attributed to its different electrostatic potential distribution. Devices modified with S-BINAPO achieved a PCE of 16.27% versus 12.65% for controls, and retained 93% of initial efficiency after 1000 h at 30-40% relative humidity.

Conclusion: Chiral phosphine oxide interface modifiers can effectively passivate buried SnO2/perovskite defects and improve both performance and durability. Among the two enantiomers, S-BINAPO is the more effective passivator, making it a promising strategy for stable HTL-free carbon-based PSCs.

Abstract: Buried interface passivation is a critical strategy to optimize the electron transport layer (ETL) and achieve high-efficiency, stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, two chiral molecules rich in benzene rings and PO groups, (R)-[1,1'-binaphthalene]-2,2'-diylbis[1,1-diphenyl-1,1'-phos] (R-BINAPO) and (S)-[1,1'-binaphthalene]-2,2'-diylbis[1,1-diphenyl-1,1'-phos] (S-BINAPO), are designed for the post-treatment of SnO2 ETLs. The PO groups act as strong Lewis bases that coordinate with undercoordinated Sn4+ on the SnO2 surface and Pb2+ on the perovskite, thereby passivating interfacial defects and suppressing non-radiative recombination. Among the two enantiomers, S-BINAPO exhibits a superior passivation effect due to its distinct electrostatic potential distribution, resulting in more effective defect passivation, enhanced carrier extraction, and reduced non-radiative recombination. As a result, hole transport layer (HTL)-free carbon-based PSC devices modified with S-BINAPO achieve a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.27%, significantly surpassing control devices (12.65%). Moreover, the S-BINAPO-modified devices show exceptional environmental stability, retaining 93% of the original PCE after aging under a relative humidity of 30-40% for 1000 h.
