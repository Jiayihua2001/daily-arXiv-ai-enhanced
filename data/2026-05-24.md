### [2] [High‐Entropy Prussian Blue Analogues as Cathodes for High‐Performance Aqueous Al‐Ion Batteries](https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.73691)
*Zengming Qin,Yang Han,Junchen Lv,Fangke Wang,Guoguo Liu,Yutong Wang,Yu Song,Kangjun Wang*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: A high-entropy Prussian blue analogue cathode is designed for aqueous aluminum-ion batteries, improving conductivity, Al3+ diffusion, and structural stability, leading to high capacity, energy density, and long cycle life.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional Prussian blue analogues suffer from poor electronic conductivity, slow Al3+ transport, and structural degradation, which limit their use in aqueous aluminum-ion batteries.

Method: The study introduces a high-entropy HE-HCF cathode by incorporating Mn, Fe, Ni, Cu, and Zn into the Prussian blue analogue framework. The resulting changes in local coordination and [Fe(CN)6] group reactivity were examined, and DFT calculations were used to evaluate electronic conductivity and Al3+ diffusion barriers. The cathode was paired with a polyvinylidene fluoride-coated Zn-Al alloy anode to assemble a full cell.

Result: The HE-HCF//Zn-Al(P) full cell delivered 110 mAh g−1 at 0.1 A g−1 and an energy density of 163.8 Wh kg−1. It also retained stable cycling for 1600 cycles at 1.0 A g−1, with improved structural stability due to reduced lattice fluctuation.

Conclusion: High-entropy engineering is an effective strategy for enhancing Prussian blue analogue cathodes in aqueous multivalent-ion batteries by simultaneously improving electrochemical activity, ion transport, and long-term structural robustness.

Abstract: Prussian blue analogues (PBAs) are promising cathodes for aqueous aluminum‐ion batteries (AAIBs) due to their 3D open frameworks, which potentially facilitate fast Al 3+ transportation. However, their practical applications are severely hindered by poor electronic conductivity, suboptimal Al 3+ diffusion kinetics, and structural degradation. This study proposes a high‐entropy Prussian blue analogue (HE‐HCF) cathode through synergistic incorporation of multiple transition metals (Mn, Fe, Ni, Cu, and Zn) to enhance aqueous Al 3+ storage. This approach alters the local coordination environment, inducing the reduction of [Fe(CN) 6 ] groups and thus enhancing their reactivity. Moreover, density functional theory (DFT) calculations reveal improved electronic conductivity and decreased Al 3+ diffusion barrier. When coupled with a polyvinylidene fluoride‐coated Zn‐Al alloy (Zn‐Al(P)) anode, the assembled HE‐HCF//Zn‐Al(P) full cell delivers a high capacity of 110 mAh g −1 at 0.1 A g −1 and a high energy density of 163.8 Wh kg −1 . Additionally, the full cell achieves a long lifespan of 1600 cycles at 1.0 A g −1 , which is attributed to the low fluctuation of lattice parameters brought from strengthened metal‐cyanide bonds within HE‐HCF. This work provides new insights into the design of multivalent ion batteries employing high‐entropy engineering.

### [3] [Dual‐Inductive and Programmable Switching: A New Paradigm in Ionic Interface‐Controlled Perovskite Memory](https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.75888)
*So‐Yeon Kim,Juan Bisquert*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: The paper presents a forming-free halide perovskite memristor with improved stability by replacing stochastic filamentary switching with a controlled, interface-driven barrier-switching mechanism.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional halide perovskite memristors suffer from random filament formation, which limits reproducibility, operational stability, and suitability for neuromorphic and analog memory applications.

Method: The authors introduce 1,4-butanediammonium iodide as an ion-modulating interfacial layer to create a rigid, cross-linked barrier at the perovskite surface. This engineered interface blocks random filament growth and shifts resistive switching to dynamic modulation of the interfacial barrier via asymmetric ion accumulation and charge-injection control.

Result: The device shows forming-free, highly reproducible, non-filamentary resistive switching with bidirectional dual-inductive responses, distinct non-zero-crossing behavior, and programmable multilevel states controlled by compliance current and operation speed.

Conclusion: Precise ionic interface engineering can suppress intrinsic stochasticity in halide perovskite memristors, enabling reliable and dynamically tunable devices for neuromorphic computing and analog memory.

Abstract: While halide perovskite (HP) memristors exhibit significant potential for artificial neural networks, their reliance on stochastic filamentary switching severely compromises operational stability. Here, we demonstrate a fully programmable, forming‐free HP memristor that overcomes this intrinsic stochasticity through precise ionic interface engineering. By employing 1,4‐butanediammonium iodide (BDAI 2 ) as an ion‐modulating layer, we construct a structurally rigid, cross‐linked interfacial network strongly anchored to the 3D HP surface, forming the grain boundaries‐modulated barrier switching. The robust ion‐blocking barrier fundamentally suppresses random filamentary growth. Resistive switching (RS) is instead governed by dynamic interfacial barrier modulation, driven by the asymmetric accumulation of mobile ions that electrostatically modulates charge injection. Crucially, this stable non‐filamentary mechanism enables highly reproducible, bidirectional dual‐inductive responses, and distinct non‐zero crossing behaviors. For the first time in an operational HP memristor, we successfully achieved these combined dynamic features. We precisely control programmable multi‐level states governed by compliance current and operation velocity. This defect‐engineered, interface‐driven approach establishes a highly reliable and dynamically controllable paradigm for advanced neuromorphic computing and analog memory architectures.
