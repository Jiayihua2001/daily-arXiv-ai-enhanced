### [2] [From Oxo to Oxyl to Biradical: Systematic Multireference Calculations of Methane Activation at MOF Nodes.](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c05408)
*Jacob J. Wardzala,Younghwan Kim,Rishu Khurana,Mukunda Mandal,Massimiliano Delferro,Cong Liu,Laura Gagliardi*

Main category: Other

TL;DR: The paper shows that methane activation at MOF node catalysts is strongly multireference in character, making standard DFT unreliable for predicting barriers and trends across 3d metals.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Methane C-H activation often involves strongly correlated electronic states that single-reference methods may not describe well. The study aims to test how dependable DFT is for these catalytic systems and to establish a more consistent multireference framework.

Method: A systematic multireference study was performed across the 3d transition-metal series using an automated active-space selection workflow. The authors applied multiconfiguration pair-density functional theory and n-electron valence state perturbation theory to compute reaction barriers and analyze electronic structure.

Result: The C-H activation step exhibits substantial static correlation. Predicted barriers differ from DFT by about 30-70 kJ mol-1, and DFT can even give incorrect barrier trends across metals. The key electronic descriptor is the M-O unit, which spans a continuum from metal-oxo to oxyl radical and O-biradical character; higher oxygen-centered spin density and weaker M-O bonding correlate with lower barriers.

Conclusion: Accurate modeling of methane activation at MOF node catalysts requires multireference methods rather than routine DFT. Catalytic activity is controlled by the electronic structure of the M-O moiety, and oxygen-centered spin density plus weakened M-O bonding provide useful descriptors for reactivity.

Abstract: Methane C-H activation at transition-metal sites often involves electronic structures that challenge conventional single-reference electronic structure descriptions. Although Kohn-Sham density functional theory (DFT) is widely used to study catalytic trends, its reliability for reactions involving strongly correlated species remains uncertain. Here we present a systematic multireference investigation of methane activation at metal-organic framework (MOF) node catalysts across the 3d transition-metal series. We introduce an automated workflow for active space selection to enable consistent application of multireference methods, including multiconfiguration pair-density functional theory and n-electron valence state perturbation theory, to these catalytic systems. These calculations show substantial static correlation in the C-H activation reaction step and predict activation barriers that differ from DFT by 30-70 kJ mol-1, with DFT often qualitatively disagreeing in barrier height trends across transition metals. Analysis of multireference wave functions shows that reactivity is governed by the electronic structure of the M-O moiety along a continuum from metal-oxo to oxyl radical and O biradical character. Increased oxygen-centered spin density and weakened M-O bonding are identified as descriptors of catalytic activity which correlate with lower activation barriers.
