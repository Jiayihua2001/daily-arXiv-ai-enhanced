Main category: Other

TL;DR: The paper reports an exceptionally large shift current in epitaxial thin films of lead-free ferroelectric CsGeI3, demonstrating a record-level bulk photovoltaic response driven by broken inversion symmetry and quantum geometry.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To explore ferroelectric halide perovskites as a platform for strong bulk photovoltaic and nonlinear optoelectronic effects, especially shift current, while avoiding lead-based materials.

Method: High-quality CsGeI3 thin films were grown by molecular beam epitaxy and characterized through photocurrent measurements under varying photon energy, light polarization, and applied electric field to verify shift-current signatures and ferroelectric switching behavior.

Abstract: Ferroelectric halide perovskites provide a fertile platform for optoelectronic functions based on the bulk photovoltaic effect, where broken inversion symmetry couples with quantum geometry of wave functions. The most prominent manifestation is the shift current, second-order nonlinear photocurrent arising from change in the Berry connection during optical transitions. Here we report a gigantic shift current response in epitaxial thin films of a lead-free ferroelectric halide perovskite CsGeI3. Remarkably, the normalized shift current magnitude surpasses those ever reported for other compounds by more than an order of magnitude, establishing a benchmark for bulk photovoltaic performance. These results identify ferroelectric halide perovskites as a powerful platform for exploring quantum-geometry-driven photoresponses and open a pathway toward next-generation photovoltaic and nonlinear optoelectronic technologies beyond the conventional junction-based architectures.

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The work aims to assess whether cubic NaCrO3 and NaMoO3 are structurally stable and to characterize their elastic, electronic, optical, and thermodynamic properties for potential multifunctional applications.

Method: The authors performed density functional theory calculations in the Wien2k framework. They optimized the cubic structures, computed elastic constants for mechanical stability, analyzed phonon dispersion and quasi-harmonic thermodynamics for dynamical and thermal stability, and evaluated electronic band structure, density of states, magnetic behavior, and optical response.

Result: NaCrO3 exhibits localized soft phonon modes, strong magnetism, and brittle mechanical behavior, though it remains mechanically stable. NaMoO3 is dynamically stable, weakly magnetic, and ductile. Both compounds are metallic and highly spin-polarized, with high low-energy reflectivity and metallic dielectric response. No thermal phase transitions were observed in the studied temperature range.

Both compounds are suggestively metallic in nature according to electronic band structure and density of states calculations, and highly spin polarized. NaCrO 3 has strong magnetism and NaMoO 3 has weak magnetism. Optical analysis shows high reflectivity in the low-energy regime and dielectric response of a metallic type. The results offer theoretical understanding of the multifunctional nature of the NaXO 3 (X = Cr, Mo) perovskites and their use in spintronic and energy storage applications.

### [6] [Discovering CO2-Reactive Carbanions via Property-Guided Generative AI.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c00680)
*Bo Li,De-en Jiang*

Main category: Generative for molecules/crystals

TL;DR: The paper combines a nucleophilicity prediction model with a generative molecular model to design new carbanions that can efficiently capture CO2 at room temperature.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Efficient CO2 chemisorption is important for reactive capture and conversion, and the authors aim to discover novel carbanions with strong CO2 reactivity using AI-guided molecular design.

Method: A directed message-passing neural network was trained on Mayr's Reactivity Database to predict nucleophilicity-related log k values, then integrated with HierVAE. The pretrained latent space was fine-tuned using the predicted property to generate candidate carbanions, followed by analysis of structural trends and DFT validation of top candidates.

Result: The approach produced structurally diverse carbanions with high generation success and strong predicted CO2 reactivity. Top candidates showed common features such as alpha-substitution and electron-withdrawing groups. DFT confirmed good agreement for electronically stabilized carbanions, while larger errors appeared for weakly stabilized or sterically unusual cases.

Conclusion: Property-guided generative AI can effectively identify promising carbanions for room-temperature CO2 chemisorption, but the predictive model has a clear applicability domain that is strongest for electronically stabilized systems.

Abstract: Designing carbanions capable of efficient CO2 chemisorption is an important approach for advancing reactive capture and conversion technologies. In this work, we integrate a nucleophilicity prediction model, trained using directed message-passing neural networks on Mayr's Reactivity Database (a large, experimentally derived collection of nucleophilicity, electrophilicity, and sensitivity parameters for organic and inorganic molecules), with the Hierarchical Variational Autoencoder (HierVAE) to generate novel carbanions. Fine-tuning the pretrained latent space with predicted log k values yields structurally diverse carbanions with strong CO2 reactivity and high generation success. Analysis of the top candidates reveals consistent trends in α-substitution, electron-withdrawing groups, and synthetic accessibility. Density functional theory (DFT) validation of the highest-ranked reactive candidates reveals good agreement for electronically stabilized systems bearing strong electron-withdrawing substituents, while deviations increase for weakly stabilized or sterically distinct carbanions, defining a clear domain of applicability for the predictive model. This study demonstrates the promise of property-guided generative AI for discovering novel carbanions for room-temperature CO2 chemisorption.
