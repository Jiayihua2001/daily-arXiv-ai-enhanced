*H. R. Enninful*

Main category: Property prediction & informatics

TL;DR: The paper audits whether domain-aligned latent representations in cross-domain materials machine learning truly become transferable. It finds only partial alignment: geometry becomes more mixed across domains, but target semantics and prediction transfer do not reliably improve.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The authors want to determine whether learned representations from heterogeneous materials datasets capture shared, transferable structure–property relationships or merely reduce domain differences without preserving useful target information.

Method: A target-free neural encoder was trained on shared physicochemical descriptors with missingness masks for porous carbons, experimental MOFs, and simulated MOFs. Domain-adversarial and noise-consistency objectives were varied to study latent alignment. The resulting representations were evaluated using neighborhood mixing, domain separability, ridge transfer probes, target-semantic probing, Spearman and partial Spearman analyses, and Ridge-probe attributions.

Conclusion: Reducing domain separability is not sufficient to produce transferable representations. Abstract: Machine learning models for materials discovery increasingly combine heterogeneous experimental and computational datasets, yet it remains unclear whether learned representations capture transferable structure–property relationships or primarily encode domain-specific biases. Here, we audit domain alignment in latent representations for cross-domain materials machine learning using experimentally measured porous carbons, experimentally measured metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), and simulated MOFs. A target-free neural encoder was trained on shared physicochemical descriptors with explicit missingness masks, while domain-adversarial and noise-consistency objectives were used to examine how alignment reshapes latent-space geometry. Across the alignment grid, the learned representations exhibited partial rather than complete alignment. Cross-domain neighbourhood mixing increased and global domain separability decreased, but local domain structure persisted. The selected aligned model achieved the strongest cross-domain neighbour mixing; however, improved geometry did not translate into improved cross-domain prediction. Ridge transfer probes showed degraded transfer performance for capacitance targets, demonstrating that reducing domain separability is insufficient to ensure transferable target semantics. Target-semantic probing further showed that electrochemical properties were encoded unevenly across latent dimensions. Spearman and domain-controlled partial Spearman analyses revealed persistent target associations, especially for volumetric capacitance, but these associations were strongly domain-dependent. Linear Ridge-probe attributions confirmed that different domains often relied on different latent coordinates for the same target property, indicating that alignment redistributed target relevance without producing a uniformly shared semantic representation. These results show that geometric alignment, target-semantic preservation, and predictive transfer are distinct properties of learned materials representations. We therefore introduce a latent-space auditing framework that combines geometry, neighbourhood mixing, target probing, transfer diagnostics, and attribution analysis to diagnose when domain alignment is only partial. This framework provides a practical route for evaluating the limits of cross-domain generalization in heterogeneous materials machine learning.

Motivation: Existing DDAP charge models can accurately capture long-range electrostatic behavior, but they depend on fixed heuristic settings and a constrained solver that may become unstable for complex or covalent materials. Method: The authors reformulate DDAP as a differentiable computational graph, enabling gradient-based optimization of Gaussian basis parameters and the reciprocal-space cutoff. Abstract: Interatomic potentials which accurately describe long-range electrostatics require atom-centered charges. One such method to determine these atom-centered charges from density functional theory (DFT) calculations is the density-derived atomic point (DDAP) charge method. DDAP fits atom-centered Gaussians to the ground-state DFT charge density and preserves the multipole moments that govern long-range electrostatics. While these charges accurately predict long-range behavior, in practice, they are limited by their reliance on fixed, heuristic parameters and a constrained solver that becomes numerically unstable for complex or covalent systems. In this work, we present opt-DDAP, which solves this limitation by reformulating the algorithm as a differentiable computational graph. This reformulation allows for the optimization of Gaussian basis parameters and the reciprocal-space cutoff using automatic differentiation. To ensure numerical robustness through this automatic differentiation process, we replace the conventional Lagrange-multiplier approach with a pseudoinverse solution followed by charge renormalization, maintaining stability even in the presence of ill-conditioned matrices. We validate the framework on NaCl vacancy supercells and on MoS2, demonstrating faithful reconstruction of both absolute and difference charge densities. The optimized charges are intended to serve as inputs to effective electrostatic models in machine learning and empirical interatomic potentials that incorporate long-range interactions.
